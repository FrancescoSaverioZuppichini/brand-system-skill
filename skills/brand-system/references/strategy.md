# Phase 1 · Strategy and phase 2 · Naming

Output: branding.md § Strategy, opening with the one line on why this brand wins for this buyer, and the pictures in the page's strategy section. It must be specific enough to constrain the visuals.

## Research the market

Stop when these exist: 6–10 competitors read and screenshotted, ≥5 buyer-voice phrases, the category cliché, the gap.

- **Competitors.** WebSearch finds them, WebFetch reads promise, audience, price and tone. WebFetch can't see colour or type, so screenshot each homepage (shoot.sh view) and note colour, type, imagery and density. Screenshots stay internal: small on the page, cropped for the logo look-alike sheet, never in brand assets, and never download competitors' images. A competitor that only exists behind a login (Instagram): note the handle and mark it ❓.
- **Adjacent leaders** (optional, 1–2): what the buyer already loves in other categories sets the quality bar. Skip them if they don't feed a decision.
- **Buyer voice.** Short phrases, each labelled *buyer's words* or *seller's view* (vendor blogs are the seller's view). Reddit and HN are often unreachable. Use:
  - software: app-store reviews, G2, Capterra, GitHub issues, forums, launch-post comments
  - consumer and local: Google Maps and TripAdvisor reviews, local directories, public social comments
- **The category cliché.** What almost everyone looks like, e.g. for dev tools "dark dashboard, violet accent, a stack trace as the hero, 'ship with confidence'". Map it to a style in `styles.md`: the one you avoid or subvert.
- **Collision check** (here only when the name is given; otherwise phase 2 owns it): the name plus the category in web search and the app stores, and `<name> app`. A near collision is a ❓, not a blocker.

Write it up as a table: competitor · who they talk to · promise · look (the screenshot) · tone · link.

## Personas

2–3, each with name, age, role, context, needs, fears and what would make them trust a new name:

| persona | needs | fears |
|---|---|---|
| Lucia, 34, orders bread in the app on her commute (customer) | it's ready when she walks past, already paid | it sold out, it's yesterday's |

The buyer and the daily user are often different people: the brand earns the buyer's trust and survives the user's eight hours a day. The client who approves the work is not a persona unless they also buy; their tastes are in brief.md.

## Gap, positioning, personality

- **The gap:** the intersection nobody owns, *who you talk to* × *how you sound* × *what you promise*. E.g. "Money apps sell growth to investors or safety to savers; nobody explains Swiss paperwork plainly to expats."
- **Positioning:** `For [target] who [need], [Brand] is the [frame of reference] that [benefit], unlike [alternative], because [proof].` Then a tagline candidate of 6 words or fewer.
- **Personality:** 3–5 adjectives, each with "means / doesn't mean", e.g. "Discreet: means private by default and a quiet UI; doesn't mean hidden pricing".

## Phase 2 · Naming (only when there is no name)

The name grows from the positioning; the vibe and the idea (phase 3) grow from the name. It goes in the strategy section of the page and of branding.md.

1. Pick 3–4 territories: the craft, the outcome, a metaphor, an invented word.
2. Write 15–30 candidates: 2–3 syllables, easy to say in the buyer's languages, spelled as they sound. Kill any with a bad meaning in a main market language.
3. Check domains and handles (Tools § Domains, § Handles) and record the method next to each result. Socials behind a login are ❓, unchecked. The user confirms at a registrar.
4. Collisions: web search and app stores in the category (GitHub and npm for dev tools), plus a rough trademark note that says it is not legal advice.
5. Shortlist 3, each with its story in one line, domain status and risks. Pick a ★, build on it and leave it ❓. If the user picks another later, re-run the wordmark and lockups; the rest holds.
6. Rename the folder to the ★'s slug and tell the user the new URL.
