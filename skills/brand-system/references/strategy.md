# Phase 1 · Strategy (and Phase 2 · Naming)

The goal of this phase is to write a single page that a stranger could read and then predict your logo, palette and headline from. If the page doesn't constrain the visuals, it isn't strategy yet.

## 1. Research the market (web search, 30–60 minutes of reading)

- Find 6–10 real competitors, plus 2–3 category leaders from adjacent markets that the buyer also uses. Read their sites, not just their names.
- For each one, note the URL, who they talk to, the promise in their own words, their visual language (colour, type, imagery, density), their price signal, and their tone.
- Write down the **category cliché**: what almost everyone looks like (for example "neon, purple gradients, sparkles, countdowns, glossy faces"). That is what the brand will avoid on purpose.
- Read where the buyers talk (Reddit, forums, reviews) to find their real fears and their words. Quote short phrases, never long passages.
- Cite everything as links. Do not download competitors' images.

Output a table: competitor · who they talk to · promise · look · tone · link.

## 2. Buyer versus user, as personas

Two or three personas, each with: name, age, role, context (size of operation, tools), what they need, what they fear, and what would make them trust a new vendor.

| persona | needs | fears |
|---|---|---|
| Marco, 31, agency owner (buyer). 12 accounts, 6 VAs | volume per account per week, one overview, output staff can run alone | quality drift customers notice, a tool that leaks or dies |

The buyer and the daily user are often different people. The brand has to earn the buyer's trust and also survive the user's eight hours a day in the product.

## 3. The gap

The gap is the intersection nobody owns: *who you talk to* × *how you sound* × *what you promise*. Example: "Generators sell to the creator's dopamine. Agency software sells to the owner, but only chat and CRM. Nobody sells *production* to the owner."

## 4. Positioning (one sentence)

`For [target] who [need], [Brand] is the [frame of reference] that [benefit], unlike [alternative], because [proof].`

Then shorten it into a tagline candidate of 6 words or fewer, for example "Same face. Every set."

## 5. Personality

3–5 adjectives. Each one gets a "means / doesn't mean" line, because adjectives without limits are useless (for example "Discreet: means private by default, quiet UI; doesn't mean hidden pricing").

## 6. The vibe and the brand idea

- **Vibe:** one metaphor sentence plus its senses: materials, light, era, sound, pace. Good examples: "A production house in Milan that runs on a terminal"; "A Renaissance atelier with a render log"; "Smoke & Signal: soft pictures, hard interface".
- **Test the vibe:** derive one colour, one typeface, one motion behaviour and one word choice from it. If you can't, rewrite it.
- **Brand idea / motif:** the single device only this brand can own. Build it from what the product does, the name, and the vibe. It will become the logo's story, the signature component and the signature motion. Write it in one line.

## 7. What the brand is NOT

List concrete clichés to ban, both visual and verbal. For example: hype, neon, purple AI gradients, ✨, countdowns, income claims, sexualised imagery, "fake Renaissance" (gilt, swashes, meme versions of famous paintings), cold blue SaaS, hacker green-on-black. Every item should be something a lazy competitor actually does.

## 8. Moodboard from your own renders

Make 4–6 tiles yourself with PIL, SVG or HTML screenshots: a type specimen, colour fields in context, a texture or material study, a composition study, and the motif in motion as a frame strip. Public-domain artworks from the brand's cultural root may appear, credited. Never paste scraped images. The moodboard proves that the vibe sentence produces a world.

## Phase 2 · Naming (only when there is no name)

1. Pick 3–4 **territories** from the strategy: the craft or technique, the outcome, a metaphor, an invented word.
2. Generate 15–30 candidates across them. Prefer 2–3 syllables, easy to say in the buyer's languages, and spelled the way it sounds.
3. Check each one:
   - **Meaning:** a negative meaning in the main market languages kills it.
   - **Domains:** check `whois` or the registrar for .com, .ai, .studio, .io.
   - **Handles:** the main socials.
   - **Collisions:** a quick web search for a same-category company with the same name, which is a rough trademark signal (say it is not legal advice).
4. Shortlist 3. For each: the story in one line, the domain status, and the risks. Mark the ★ pick and leave the choice as ❓ for the user.
